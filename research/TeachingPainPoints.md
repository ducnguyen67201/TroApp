# Teaching pain points

This log records firsthand classroom experiences and early product ideas. Ideas
are discussion starters, not agreed requirements or implemented capabilities.

## Students falling behind during a live teaching session

Recorded: October 7, 2026. Source: Duc's account of teaching as a TA today.

### Reported experience

- Many students could not follow the teaching quickly enough while working on an activity.
- Students frequently asked the TA for help, interrupting class time.
- The repeated interruptions were frustrating for the TA and disrupted the teaching flow.

The report does not yet establish why students fell behind, how many were affected,
or which kinds of questions caused the interruptions.

### Need to explore

Help a student understand the current activity and catch up at their own pace,
while allowing the TA to continue teaching. Students should still be able to ask
the TA when they need human help.

### Early idea from the TA

Let a student quickly grab context from a screen and receive an explanation there,
possibly added directly onto the screen. The intended screen, capture method and
presentation format are still unclear. This could mean the student's work, the
TA's demonstration, or both; do not choose one before discussing the experience.

### Questions for the next discussion

- In one concrete interruption, what was the TA demonstrating and where did the student get stuck?
- Did the student miss a step, misunderstand a concept, or encounter a different result on their own screen?
- Which context would explain the gap: the TA's current step, earlier steps, the student's screen, or class materials?
- What would useful help look like in that moment: a short explanation, a replay, an annotation, or guided steps?

### Possible success signals to validate

Students can resume the activity with understanding; the TA spends less class time
repeating individual explanations; unresolved questions still reach the TA.
These are proposed outcomes, not measured results.
